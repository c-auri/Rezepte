---
title: "Shorbet Adas - Lebanese Lentil Soup"
---
This kind of lentil soup is a staple in the whole arabic world and I imagine every family has their own recipe that's slighly different. Looking through the internet I found many different variations for the same dish, but as a non-Arab I have an unbiased perspective and am therefore qualified to judge what's the best recipe of them all of course. The defining characteristic of the soup is the use of red or yellow lentils, a heavy emphasis on cumin as a spice base and bright lemon on top of it all. Everything else is a matter of personal preference.

Some recipes add carrots or celery. Some just sweat the onions lightly, others aggressively brown them and even add them *after* blending so they stay crispy. Some add rice, some potato, some don't use any additional starch at all. And I'm sure there are a dozen other variants. I personally like slowly frying the onions until golden brown. This brings a sweet and deep flavor base that works really well together with the cumin. Adding other vegetables doesn't really add anything worthwhile in terms of flavor if you ask me. Rice or potato is nice for improved texture though, but not essential.

Cooking the onions is a long process. I will usually stop when about half of them are browned as a compromise, but that still takes a while. Frying them in a separate skillet from the lentils speeds up the overall cooking time because you can do the two time-intesive steps in parallel, but it means there is one more pot to clean. Otherwise there isn't really a difference to cooking everything in the same pot, so pick whatever is more convenient.

In terms of spices, ground cumin is the most important one. It gives a strong earthy base that I'm not sure you can get with just whole cumin seeds. So if you have to buy it, prioritize preground. If you just have whole cumin, grind some of it up yourself and then use a mix of the grinds and some additional whole seeds as well. Turmeric is mostly there for color, you can skip it entirely if you don't have it.

You can use vegetable stock or whatever kind of instant bouillon you like to cook the lentils in, but using just water is honestly fine. I always just eyeball the amount of liquid, it should be about and inch or two above the lentils depending on the size of your pot. The lentils will soak up most of it and significantly increase in size. You can always add more water later if it gets too dry.

Traditionally this dish was simmered for a very long time until the lentils completely fell apart. Nowadays it's much easier to just blend everything together with a stick blender directly in the pot once the lentils are cooked. You can also use a regular kitchen blender if that's what you have.

## Ingredients

- 2 cups red or yellow lentils, or a mix of both
- 1 medium to large yellow onion, chopped
- 1 tablespoon ground cumin
- 1&thinsp;-&thinsp;2 lemons, zested (optional) and juiced, usually 1 is enough but the amount of juice varies so it's better to have backup
- water or vegetable stock
- oil (ideally extra virgin olive oil, but a neutral plant oil will also do)
- salt and black pepper 
- optional:
    - ½ teaspoon turmeric
    - 1 garlic clove, finely diced
    - 1 diced small potato or 2 tablespoons of rice
    - 1 teaspoon MSG (not traditional but I think it goes really well)
    - parsley

## Preparation

- Optional: Soak lentils in cold water for 10&thinsp;-&thinsp;30 minutes. This reduces cooking time and makes them more digestible. But you can skip it and just cook for longer. It's a good option for when you want to do everything in one pot and have to wait for the onions anyways. If you do soak them, discard and replace the cloudy water before cooking.
- Slowly fry onion over low to medium heat while stirring every once in a while, should develop some golden brown color but not burn. Will take about 10&thinsp;-&thinsp;20 minutes. Using higher heat makes it go faster but requires more frequent stirring.
- When the onions are done, add garlic, cumin and turmeric for about a minute until fragrant.
- Cook lentils in unsalted water or vegetable stock in a lidded pot until soft, 10&thinsp;-&thinsp;30 minutes depending on what kind and if they were soaked. 
    - Optional: Skim off the foam that builds up when bringing the water to a boil. This will slightly improve the overall texture and makes the dish more digestable.
    - When available, add diced potato or rice and cook together with lentils.
    - Either cook lentils directly in the same pot you cooked the onions in, or add onion-spice mix to lentils once it is done.
- When lentils are completely soft: Kill the heat, add lemon zest and blend everything until smooth. Adjust texture with fresh water if it get's too dry. Should be on the thicker side of soupy. 
- Season with salt, MSG, pepper and lemon juice to taste. Lentils can take quite a bit of salt, so don't be shy but taste as you go.
- Serve just as is, or finish with some chopped parsley, olive oil and freshly ground pepper on top.

While already delicious when served right away, the soup gets even better after it had some rest. So making enough to have leftovers is a good idea. Keeps in the fridge for a couple of days. The lentils will draw some more moisture over time, so add some water and reseason with salt when reheating.
